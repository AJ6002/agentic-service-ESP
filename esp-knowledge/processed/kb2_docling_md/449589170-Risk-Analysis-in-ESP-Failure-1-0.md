## Abstract:

In oil industry, ESP (Electric Submersible Pump) failure risks play a significant part in decision-making and may affect the performance of the production and additional operation  costs.  Moreover,  ESP  always  face  uncertainty  that  leads  to  cost  and schedule risks. Most of those risks can be mitigated with better up-front planning and analysis.

In  this  study,  risks  associated  to  more  than  282  ESPs  operating  at  Akakus  Oil Company in El- Sharara field, producing fluids from three concession, over time the number of production wells has increased, and additional ESPs have brought on line, which make the number of failures increasing with time. The qualitative risk analysis  process  carried  results  by  risk  3x3  matrix  that  helped  us  to  rate  the significance of our identified risks based on the probability of the risk materializing and the impact if it does. The results showed a reduction on ESP risk failures and risk responses reduce the action of likelihood of occurrence or the impact of risks.

## Introduction:

Electric Submersible Pump (ESP) failures have important economic implications for O&amp;G  operators,  including  nonproductive  time,  production  deferral,  production losses,  and  high  cost  of  ESP  replacements.  Since  the  birth  of  ESP  in  1926  the technology  has  not  evolved  significantly  and  the  technology  of  today  has  many similarities with the original ESP system designed in 1926.Today approximately 10% of the world oil production is produced with electrical submerged pumping. ESP are also  known for lifting much greater liquid rates than most of the other types of artificial lift, both on-and offshore. Considering the large number of ESPs deployed in an oilfield, the bulk of ESP failure prediction research has focused on computing and estimates causes of failure. Statistical analysis of ESP failures across an oilfield has shown that the failure rates vary considerably.

Submersible pumps (ESPs) consist of multiple centrifugal pump stages mounted in series  within  a  housing  mated  to  a  submersible  electric  motor.  These  pumps connected to surface controls and electric power by armor-protected cables (Bernt H, 2010).

## Literature reviews:

Many previous studies have been conduct within the field of integrated cost and schedule risk analysis but each presents a different approach to this concept. ## Risk Management Methodologies:

Project Management Institute (PMI) defined risk as an uncertain event or condition that, if it occurs, has a positive or negative effect on one or more project objectives such as scope, schedule, cost, and quality (PMBOK Guide, 2013). A risk may have one or more causes and, if it occurs, it may have one or more impacts. A cause may be  a  given  or  potential  requirement,  assumption,  constraint,  or  condition  that creates the possibility of negative or positive outcomes. For example, causes could include the requirement of an environmental permit to do work, or having limited personnel assigned to design the project.

The study used different ways to identify risks, such as documentation review, daily reports,  ESP  pull  reports,  information  gathering  (Brainstorming  with  drilling  and production engineers, interviewing with Schlumberger supervisor, petroleum active supervisor, and Production supervisor at Akakus Company). ## Overview of identifying Risk in ESP Pump Failure:

## Risks related to Completion Failure:

1. Failure of perforations or liner.
2. Failure in the sand control system.

3. Wellbore completion failure. Failure of the wellbore completion for example: Casing, tubing, packer, safety valve or liner failures.

## Risks related to Installation:

1. System assembly such as Mounting of the equipment.
2. Reran damaged equipment.
3. Well cleanout.
4. ESP field service such as Testing Well optimization.

## Risks related to Manufacturing:

1. Wrong material selection.
2. Improper fabrication or assembly of Components.
3. Improper equipment testing or quality control.

## Risks related to Operation:

1. Inadequate condition monitoring.
2. Poor operating procedure for example if it is performed unnecessary many start/stop.
3. Well treatment like Injection of inhibitors.

## Risks related to Wear-and-Tear:

1. Pump worn, Broken or fractured.
2. Motor contaminated, corroded.
3. Cable low resistance.
4. Seal contaminated, corroded.
5. Pump Intake, Broken or fractured.

## Risks related to Reservoir or Fluids:

1. Presence of asphaltene in the well fluid.
2. Bottom-hole temperature above approximately 80°C.
3. Free gas in well fluid.
4. Sand content in well fluid.
5. Scale formation.
6. High water cut in the well fluid.
7. Low or no inflow of fluids in the well.

## Risks related to System Design / Selection:

1. Wrong selection of ESP equipment for the given application such as.
2.  Inadequate pump flow or head capacity.
3.  Motor power capacity
4.  Use of low-grade materials
2. Improper system design / selection, including use of improper data or errors in calculations.

## Pump parties that can damage and cause ESP failure:

## Electrical Cable:

## The possible causes of cable failures are:

-  Mechanical damage (crushing, cutting, etc.) during running or pulling operations .
-  Corrosion .
-  Insulation deterioration due to high temperature or wellbore gases .
-  Currents above the design limit can rapidly increase cable temperature leading to insulation breakdown .

## PUMP:

## Typical root causes to pump failures listed in the following:

-  Up-thrust wear occurs when the pump operated at flow rates greater than the maximum recommended flow rate. In floating-type pumps the impellers forced up against the diffusers, the up thrust washers may be overloaded and it can result in a destroyed pump stage. Solids production accelerates the process.
-  Down-thrust wear typically occurs at flow rates lower than the minimum recommended rate also accelerated by solids production. Down-thrust washers destroyed after the pump stage destroyed.
-  Scale formation can plug or even lock pump stages.

## Motor:

1. Overloading of the motor leads to heating of the motor's wiring; can lead to damage or burnout. Overload is usually caused by:
2.  Undersized motor.
3.  High specific gravity of the well fluid can cause the actual total dynamic head (TDH) to be greater than assumed.
4.  Pump failures that result in increased power requirement.
5.  Irregular (high, low, or unbalanced) voltage at the motor terminal.
2. Leaking protectors allow well fluids to enter the motor causing a gradual contamination of the motor oil, which can lead to short circuit or motor burnout.
3. Insufficient cooling . Cooling is insufficient when; flow velocity past the motor is lower than required for an efficient transfer of the generated heat from the motor.

## Seal Failures:

Seal section failures may attributed to the following factors:

1. Broken or damaged mechanical seals in the seal section can cause well fluids to leak into the motor. This can be a result of:
2.  Vibrations transmitted from a worn pump.
3.  Wrong equipment selection or improper installation.
2. The ESP main thrust bearing in the seal may fail when the pump operates in excessive up or down-thrust mode.
3. Labyrinth-type seals may fail in deviated (more than 30 degrees) well sections.

## Shaft Failures:

1. Torsional yield failure occurs when the torque limit of the shaft exceeded. Can result in permanent deformation or a break of the shaft.
2. Torsional twist in the shaft absorbs the energy during system start-up. If the shaft twists more than allowed by a connected part then that part can be damage.
3. Bearing wear mostly occurs when solids produced when clearances in radial bearings increase. This will weaken the shaft stability and vibrations will increase.
4. Lost lubrication in radial bearings of the pump can happened because of the metal-to-metal rubbing create increased local temperatures, and bearing materials weld together. This can lead to a damaged or broken shaft, (Bernt H, 2010).

The risk 3x3 matrix helps us to rate the significance of our identified risks based on the probability of the risk materializing and the impact if it does. There are various sizes and types of risk matrix, depending on the rating scale chosen. The simplest form uses a 3×3 grid, but you could use a 4×4, a 5×5 or a 6×6 grid, depending on how complicated or simple you want to make things. In reality, it does not really matter. Because when we look at probability and impact, we are really trying to determine the most significant risks, in other words, the ones that are most likely to happen and the ones with the most serious consequences .

## Quantitative risk analysis:

Quantitative  Risk  Analysis  is  the  process  of  numerically  analyzing  the  effect  of identified risks on overall project objectives. The key benefit of this process is that it  produces  quantitative  risk  information  to  support  decision  making  in  order  to reduce project uncertainty (PMBOK Guide, 2013).

This is a numerical analysis of the probability (P) and impact (I) of the risk on the project. Determine overall project risk (risk exposure), identify risks requiring the most  attention,  determine  cost  and  schedule  reserves,  and  create  realistic  and achievable cost, schedule, or scope targets (Tawfik R et al, 2015).

Risk Score Value (Rating) = P × I

## Reservoir or Fluids Plan Risks Responses and Resulting:

Reservoir or fluids risks responses defined as the set of actions that seek to reduce the likelihood of occurrence or the impact of risks, we will examine the different categories of actions and controls that can employed.

Bottom hole temperature above 80c : High well temperatures can have detrimental effect on the motor, which can overheat and burn out. Here it is important to select a pump that can handle the well temperature.

Free gas in the well fluid: Formation gas can lead to gas lock in the pump. By utilize proper gas handlers or separators one will be able to a certain degree withstands this threat. Free gas can also migrate into the power cable; to mitigate this one can select a cable with proper insulation.

Sand content in well fluid: Sand or abrasive production problems can be avoid or reduce by selecting a proper pump type and materials.

Scale  formation: Scale  deposits  on  motor  housings  will  obstruct  the  motor  in getting enough cooling effect from the well fluid, which can result in motor burn out.  Special  coatings  applied  to  the  motor  housing  are  the  usual  solution.  Scale formation in pump stages reduces the liquid rate and can result in a plugged pump.

High water cut in the well fluid: To reduce this problem Select motor and pumps with the proper specifications that capable of deliver the desired flow rate.

## Conclusion:

This  study represents the application of risk management process  in Oil Filed at Akakus  Oil  Operations  Company.  It  has  been  identifying  that  ESP  failures  often caused by electric errors because of insufficient protection of the electric motor, pump and cable, which results in a motor failure.  The A-field in The Akakus Oil Company will subjected to initial sand production, in addition high production rates and  high  water  cut  lead  to  increased  sand  production  during  the  field  life. Compression pumps handle solids production better than floater pump because it is no contact between impellers and diffusers under operation. Moreover, material selection is an important issue to consider. To improve reliability one has to choose materials,  which  designated  to  its  application  area.  This  study  depending  on reducing the cost probability by a factor of two is achievable at a manageable cost to mitigate the ESP failures and reducing Workover operations that leads to high cost.
