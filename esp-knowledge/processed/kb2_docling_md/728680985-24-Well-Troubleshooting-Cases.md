## Gas Locking in ESP motor shroud Summary of Findings

- square4 ESP has 7' shroud (6.18' ID) attached with gravel pack screens below motor (5.62' OD).
- square4 Pumping running normally until 23:00 hrs when pump ∆ P starts to decrease slowly.
- -No change in pump discharge pressure indicates no change of fluid density in tubing.
- -Confirmed no change to motor speed.
- -Slow decrease in pump ∆ P points to increasing quantities of free gas in pump.
- -Pump continues to process gas until 08:00 hrs when the high motor temperature shut down protects the ESP
- square4 After pump shut down, fluid level in tubing falls hence drop in discharge pressure.
- square4 Pump restarted at 10:00 hrs but same rapid rise in motor temperature

## Diagnosis

- circle4 Loss of pump ∆ P indicates presence of free gas.
- circle4 Fluid bubble point is approximately 30 psia below the lowest intake pressure observed. However inside the motor shroud with heavy oil, low heat transfer and elevated skin temperatures bubble point increases with temperature and so free gas emerges inside the shroud.
- circle4 Pump can handle this small amount of gas without gas locking.
- circle4 But small annulus (0.25') between ESP motor and shroud fills with gas, stopping heat transfer away from motor.

The example trend below shows a standard ESP with gas separator running in a well with high free gas content. The well is horizontal and 'burps' large slugs of gas, which upset the ESP operation. The only way to control these slugs was to quickly choke back the wellhead pressure as discharge pressure was seen to drop off (thereby compressing some of the discharge pressure of the pump. ## Using the MultiSensor data on high GOR wells

This example shows the effect of a well being produced with an intake pressure below bubble point. It can be seen that the pressure trend oscillates as the volume of gas at the pump intake increases. The oscillation occurs as the pump ∆ P decreases due to the lighter density free gas. As the intake pressure rises gas is forced back into solution and the pump delta increases again, producing a lower intake pressure. The total oscillation period is approximately 3 minutes in this case.

## Reducing Vibrations

Trend vibration data has shown that certain ESPCP operating frequencies can produce high vibration readings.

Adjusting the operating frequency by a small amount up or down can significantly reduce the vibration value, reducing the mechanical stress on the downhole completion. The plot also shows the effect on motor temperature as the frequency is reduced. Power consumption of the pump is proportional to the frequency of operation; therefore reducing the speed of the pump reduces the load on the motor.

An identical phenomenon occurs with ESP and PCP pumps which are frequency controlled.
