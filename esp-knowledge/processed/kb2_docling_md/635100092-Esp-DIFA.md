## The ESP Industry

- The ESP industry is littered with failures that are repetitive
- Many (maybe even most) are avoidable
- ESP reputation is 'high-volume' - but not always reliable
- Some operators will sacrifice production because of this reputation
- So where do we do things go wrong?

## A Few Common Misconceptions

1. All ESPs are the same
2. Top quality will be delivered
3. Failure analysis will be objective and without bias
4. Short cuts will not be taken

## Common Deficiencies

Often, operators fail to recognize the value of focused attention on ESP quality and reliability.  Often, focus only occurs after a failure(s).

1. Operators do not possess the in-house skill set
- a) Failure to follow proper/established protocols
- b) Failure to follow protocols leads to loss of information and therefore the ability to improve
2. When someone is the 'designated' ESP person, they may not be qualified in all areas of the life cycle
- a) E.g.; may understand ESP applications, but are not qualified as manufacturing and reliability expert
3. Assume that the goals are aligned between operator and supplier

## Common Deficiencies

4. The nuances, or differences, between suppliers can be significant
5. Third party supplied items often are overlooked
6. Repeated failures (don't learn from a previous mistake)
7. Inability to have an overview of strengths and weakness between suppliers/equipment

## Closing

1. Conducting failure analysis requires focused attention from 'someone' - PDO's effort is the best example of focused improvement
2. Detailed and repeatable methods should be applied
3. Suppliers and Operators do not always share the same goals
4. Proper training and utilization of technology is imperative.
5. Proper focus on all aspects of the life cycle should produce improvement for all parties
