## Abstract

This paper outlines an integrated process and methods in troubleshooting Electrical Submersible Pump (ESP) downtime by identifying the trips root cause through a systematic approach whereas more than 15 surface and subsurface parameters are thoroughly analysed. Moreover, the paper discussed how the behaviour  and  markers  of  these  combined  parameters  can  result  in  each  ESP  trip  scenario.  The  best applicable remedy actions are laid out for each separate case taking into considerations maintaining ESP system mechanical and electrical integrity.

More than 15 surface and subsurface related parameters are thoroughly analysed and the behaviour of the combined effects of each parameter on each other is utilized to identify the multiple trip markers. Through trip markers analysis, more than 10 general trip causes were classified with a clear process and workflow to distinguish each case during the troubleshooting process. Moreover, specific causes in each general case are also discussed in this paper to further distinguish and identify the exact trip cause. The paper also outlines the recommended remedy action to be taken to return the ESP back online with minimized downtime.

ESP trip  is  defined  as  any  unwanted  shutdown  of  ESP  system  through  the  activation  of  ESP  drive protection setpoint due to internal or external fault or situation. Any component in the production system from reservoir up-to processing facilities can impact ESP systems negatively. Similarly, any component in the electrical system from motor up-to electrical substation can impact it with the same intensity as well. For these reasons, multiple factors can lead to an ESP trip leading to a time-consuming process to identify the root cause and returning the ESP online. The results discussed in this paper shows that the exact root cause can be promptly identified through the mentioned methodology with high accuracy leading to successful restarting of ESP system in optimized operating point and conditions.

The results  and  conclusions  discussed  in  this  paper  can  be  utilized  to  successfully  troubleshoot  any tripped ESP by accurately identifying the exact root cause through the discussed markers. The recommended remedy actions explained in this paper ensures the ESP is back online with minimal downtime with an optimized performance that maintains its electrical and mechanical integrity.

Keywords: Artificial Lift, ESP, gas-lock, ESP run-life, downtime, ESP trips, ESP failure, data analysis

## Introduction

## ESP Failure

ESP  systems  are  composed  of  various  components  with  mechanical  and  electrical  aspects.  These components contain multiple elements each with different objective and purpose. As such, ESP systems can fail due to multiple reasons and by many casual failure factors. Identifying ESP failure root causes is done through a comprehensive dismantle, inspection, and failure analysis (DIFA) process (Pessoa, et al. 2024).

Electrical Failure. Electrical failures occur when the motor fails, not being able to fully or partially spin due to compromised electrical integrity in any part of the ESP electrical system. Most commonly due to phaseto-ground faults or phase-to-phase faults. In such conditions the power supplied will not met the threshold necessary for the motor to provide the required load; and motor voltage and current will be unbalanced across the three-phases and/or vary in multiple folds above or below the motor rated specifications.

Mechanical Failure. Mechanical failures occur when the mechanical energy is not transferred fully or partially from the motor to the pump or any part thereof. It can be sub-classified further into below main classifications.

## Stuck Shaft

This type of mechanical failure occurs when the motor fails to rotate due to the shaft or any other mechanical component getting stuck. This results in motor drawing high amperage until the shaft is released, breaks, or the system fails electrically due to the high electrical stress.

## Broken Shaft

This type of mechanical failure occurs when the motor rotates but the mechanical energy is not transmitted to the pump due to broken mechanical components or mechanical disengagement between them. The motor continues to rotate but operates in underload conditions as no load is exhibited in the motor (inability to lift fluids).

## Underperformance

This  type  of  mechanical  failure  occurs  when  the  flow  at  surface  is  not  as  designed  based  on  pump performance curve due to mechanical underperformance in the ESP system. Most commonly occurs in aged systems or those in gassy or sandy environments as the stages get damaged over time thus losing their efficiency (Boudi, et al. 2024).

## Pump failure

This occurs when the pump and all associated parts (stages; diffusers and/or impellers) get damaged and unable to lift the required fluids. This mode of failure can be expressed in different scenarios of completely wear of pump stages (fully or partially) as in the case of diffusers spinning or impellers non-spinning. Such events might occur due to: manufacturing defects or foreign/incompatible fluid type productions.

## ESP Trip

ESP trip is defined as any unwanted shutdown of ESP system through the activation of ESP drive protection setpoint due to internal or external fault or situation. Thus, any shutdown other than manual ESP shutdown - locally or remotely - is considered an ESP trip. ESP trips vary in complexity based on the root cause of the trip from basic trips that ESP can be restarted after with no changes in parameters to very complex trips that require significant enhancements and optimization in ESP operating conditions. To recognize which part of the spectrum each trip falls under and the required steps to overcome, the most critical item is to identify the exact root cause of the trip. This not only ensures production continuity and reduce deferred revenue due to offline ESP system but also ensures the ESP integrity is maintained by reducing the repetitive trips and avoiding operating the ESP system in unfavorable conditions.

## Protection Setpoints

Protection Setpoints are user-defined settings in VSD to allow for automatic ESP shutdown without human intervention under specific scenarios. They are commonly called "Trip Setpoints" as they lead to an ESP trip. The term "Trip" means any ESP shutdown due to unforeseen conditions. Basically, all ESP shutdowns other than Planned Shutdown -- manual local or remote stop. These setpoints ensure healthy ESP integrity as operating the ESP system in such conditions leads to ESP failure. There are many Protection Setpoints but they all primarily maintain ESP integrity by targeting the following two conditions.

Motor Overheat. The primary mean of cooling the motor during its operation is the heat transfer between the motor and the produced fluid. Lack of flow across the motor leads to significant motor -and other ESP components- overheat and mechanical ware due to operating outside the pump recommended operation range (RoR).

Electrical  Overload. The  motor  windings  are  rated  for  specific  amperage  based  on  the  motor  type and model. Exceeding the nameplate amperage compromises the electrical integrity of the system due to significant overheat of the electrical components.

## Motor Load

Motor Load is defined as  the  percentage  of  operating  motor  horsepower  in  reference  to  its  nameplate horsepower. However, since amps values is readily available and continuously measured by the drive and since the relationship between HP load and amps load is relatively linear and nearly 1:1 with HP load at higher loads then the motor load definition is used as the latter not the former.

During ESP design and operation, the common practice is to have the minimum motor load to be 50%. This is because the efficiency and power factor of the system drops significantly below 50%. The absolute minimum motor load is the idle motor load which is around 30%-40% motor load (Williams 2019).

## Underload

The motor converts the electrical energy to mechanical energy. The mechanical energy is transferred from the shaft onto the pump shaft and then is imparted on the produced fluid. Hence, the amount of required electrical energy by the motor is directly proportional and dependent of the generated hydraulic energy. If there is no flow generated by the pump the motor load would be much less and thus is "underloaded". The underload setpoint is commonly set at 80%-90% of running amps (Camilleri, et al. 2018) or amps value during deadhead condition. Most common causes for Underloading the motor: Blockage in flow path above pump discharge (scale build-up, downhole plug/BPV, closed SSSV/SSV, closed valve in wellhead or flowline), gas lock, Broken Shaft, Damaged Stages, Wrong Rotation, etc.

## Pump Discharge Pressure

Pump discharge pressure is inversely proportional to flowrate and directly proportional to produced fluid specific gravity and frequency. Thus, an increase in discharge pressure is a common indication of no-flow across the motor hence leading to motor overheat. The discharge pressure setpoint is commonly set at 150 psi - 300 psi above running discharge pressure. Most common causes for High Discharge Pressure: Blockage in flow path above pump discharge (scale build-up, downhole plug/BPV, closed SSSV/SSV, closed valve in wellhead or flowline), changes in produced fluid composition, etc.

## Motor Temperature

Heat transfer with fluid flowing across the motor is the primary cooling mechanism for the ESP motor and with no flow (or less flow than anticipated) the motor will significantly overheat and burn. The motor temperature  setpoint  depends  on  motor  and  dielectric  oil  types.  Most  common  causes  for  high  motor temperature: Blockage in flow path above pump discharge (scale build-up, downhole plug/BPV, closed SSSV/SSV, closed valve in wellhead or flowline), changes in produced fluid composition, gas lock, running with higher motor current values (e.g. higher feed amperage) etc.

## Overload

Motor load is directly proportional to produced flowrate -- and generated head. While higher flowrate leads to better cooling of the motor there will be a tipping point whereas the increase in frequency and increase in running amps will generate more heat leading to motor overheat. Running frequency, fluid specific gravity, and system electrical integrity all can lead to overload as well. The overload setpoint is commonly set at 110%-115% of nameplate amps (Awaid, et al. 2014) and can be set to maximum of 120% for troubleshooting purposes. Most common causes for Overload: High running frequency, wrong sized motor, stuck shaft, changes in produced fluid composition, compromised system electrical integrity.

## Diagnosis

Broken shaft below the pump.

## Markers

No Load on Motor: Very low motor amperage; motor load near idle conditions; frequency changes don't affect the amperage; motor draws same amperage at both rotations.

No flow: increase in motor temperature; no change in intake temperature, equalized intake pressure and discharge pressure, and static wellhead pressure.

Mechanical Issue: high fluctuations in vibration. Remedy

Follow Failure Declaration Workflow and arrange for ESP system replacement.

## Diagnosis

Full Downhole Circulation.

## Markers

No Flow to Surface: Low WHP; Pd equal to Pi; no surface flowrate; low WHT.

Downhole Circulation: Low differential pressure; high Tm; significant increase in Tm after shutdown.

Healthy Shaft: High motor amps and load; amps react to changes in frequency.

## Remedy

Rigless Job: Replace Y-Tool through WL.

Rig Job: Replace Downhole tubing and completion.

## Recommendations

Due to the complexity of ESP systems and the different electrical and mechanical components in the system composition, they can trip due to multiple casual factors. These causal factors can vary in their complexity as well and they can be of internal cause (ESP related) and external cause (environment related). To avoid further production interruption due to the ESP trip and reduce the repetitive trips, it is recommended to follow the discussed troubleshooting workflow to ensure reaching accurate conclusion about the exact root cause of ESP trip and identify it promptly. Moreover, if all troubleshooting attempts fail to return the ESP back on-stream then it is recommended to follow the failure declaration workflow to ensure accurate ESP status classification to avoid improper classification of its status.

For future work, it is recommended to:

- Incorporate these workflows as algorithms in software tools to automate the process. The results obtained from the automation tools can be benchmarked with the manual process results to assess and validate its accuracy.
- Explore building a machine learning model and feeding it the behaviour of the subsurface and surface  parameters  before  ESP  trip  for  the  cases  discussed  in  this  paper  as  a  next  step  in  the automation process.

## Summary

This paper discussed the different major conditions that can result in ESP downtime. These conditions can further impact ESP mechanical and electrical integrity negatively leading to ESP failure. As such, the paper outlined two workflows that are recommended be employed by artificial lift engineers while troubleshooting ESP trips.

The methods and procedures in those workflows are pivotal in ensuring a steady and continuous decrease in deferred production and increase in ESP systems runlife by significantly reducing repetitive trips caused by improper trip identification. This significant reduction in trips ensures the longevity of ESP population as it protects their mechanical and electrical integrity. The positive impact of such procedures can be tracked through select ESP key-performance-indicators (KPI) (Al-Khalaf, et al. 2020) with immediate results in the first year and significant improvements by third year of implementation as evident by below graph.

ESP trips can be challenging to analyze and rectify; they also might require the ESP to be shut-in for extended periods for the troubleshooting duration. As such, reducing these trips through following these workflows can have a major impact on the field production strategies. It is worth noting that, ESP frequent shutdowns and start-ups (normal start/stop or tripping) can impact and impose higher stresses on the integrity of the systems (Albori, et al. 2024).
